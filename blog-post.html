How to Write a Resume That Gets You Noticed

Admin
•
Jan 05, 2026
•
5 min read

In today's competitive job market, your resume is often the only chance you get to make a first impression on a recruiter or hiring manager. With only an average of 6 seconds spent on the initial scan, your resume needs to be clear, concise, and compelling.

"Your resume isn't just a history of your past work—it's an advertisement for your future potential."

1. Choose the Right Format

Chronological resumes are the standard, but functional or hybrid formats can work well if you're changing careers or have employment gaps. However, for most professionals, showing your experience from most recent to oldest is usually the best approach.

2. Pass the ATS (Applicant Tracking System)

Most large companies use ATS software to scan resumes for keywords. To ensure your resume gets through:

  • Use standard fonts like Inter, Arial, or Calibri.
  • Avoid complex graphics or tables.
  • Include keywords directly from the job description.
  • Save your final file as a PDF unless requested otherwise.

3. Focus on Achievements, Not Just Duties

Instead of just listing what you did, show how well you did it using the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Use metrics whenever possible to quantify your success.

Weak: Responsible for managing social media accounts.

Strong: Increased social media engagement by 45% over 6 months by implementing a new content strategy and managing a KSh 50,000 monthly ad budget.

4. Keep it Concise

Aim for 1-2 pages maximum. Be ruthless about cutting out irrelevant experience from over 10 years ago unless it's highly relevant to the role you're applying for.
